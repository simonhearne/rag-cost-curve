PRE-REGISTRATION SUMMARY. Everything below was fixed in writing before
any WS9 row existed, so every verdict is a lookup rather than a
judgement made after seeing the data. This block exists because the
spec under docs/ is stripped at public release and this file is then
the only pre-registration artefact that survives.

  HYPOTHESES (spec section 3).
  H1  the saving widens with corpus size. Agentic cost rises with how
      much tree it must search; indexed cost is set by a fixed batch
      of ranked chunks and should be flatter. So c_live - c_idx grows
      with size and Q* falls with size.
  H2  at S the agentic arm may be CHEAPER than the index, making Q*
      infinite there. A 49-file tree is cheap to grep. What H2 firing
      would establish is that size dependence exists on an
      uncontaminated corpus too -- that grep's advantage is partly a
      function of how much tree there is, on a corpus with a 0.000
      parametric floor. H2 is the hypothesis this design exists to be
      able to lose to, and the report states its outcome whichever way
      it falls. H2 firing does NOT make scale an alternative
      explanation for the fastapi result: grep wins on fastapi at
      6,232,509 count_tokens and loses on agentic-hil at 4,307,363, so
      no monotone size effect produces that ordering. Contamination is
      settled by the matched-size C-series, not by H2.
  H3  the fixed cost does not move. Under the headline infra mode
      (dedicated r8g.large, pca_uc_384_sq8) the index's fixed daily
      cost is flat across all three sizes, so the shape of this curve
      is set entirely by the per-query saving; the footprint is
      load-bearing only in marginal mode.

  BRANCHES (spec section 4). Cells outside these series emit
  'n/a (not pre-registered)', and no letter refers to anything but the
  quantity its series names.

  F -- finiteness of break-even on the unseen corpus, over the agentic
       vs indexed pair at the headline infra mode and footprint,
       Claude regime.
    F1  break_even_qpd finite at S, M AND L.
    F2  finite at some sizes and infinite at others; the report names
        which.
    F3  infinite at every size (live cheaper per query throughout).

  B -- the abstract's band, only over sizes where F gives a finite Q*.
    B1  every finite Q* inside [10, 1000) queries/day.
    B2  at least one finite Q* outside it; the report names the size
        and the number.

  K -- the top-k ordering across the curve.
    K1  Q*(indexed, k=10) < Q*(indexed_topk3, k=3) at every size: the
        order the deck quotes at L is preserved across the curve.
    K2  the order reverses at some size: the two cross below L.
    K3  at least one size has an infinite Q* on one arm, so the pair
        is not ordered there.

  U -- per-size paired token deltas, resolved independently for each
       (contrast, size) cell over prompt_tokens ARM CONTRASTS ONLY.
       Contrasts: indexed - agentic, indexed_topk3 - agentic,
       indexed_topk3 - indexed.
    U1  95% CI excludes zero and is negative (the index arm costs
        fewer tokens).
    U2  CI excludes zero and is positive.
    U3  CI includes zero: report the point estimate with its interval
        and claim NEITHER direction.
    judge_correct and turns deltas are computed and published as
    supporting mechanism and carry 'n/a (not pre-registered)'. No
    U-letter anywhere refers to anything but a prompt_tokens delta.
    The per-arm size-span deltas (L-S, L-M within an arm) are
    exploratory and carry 'n/a (not pre-registered)' too.

  C -- the matched-size contamination test, over agentic vs indexed
       alone (results/ws6c/matched_size_contamination.csv). Two
       scopes are matched iff their measured count_tokens are within
       2.0x of each other; pairing is by measured tokens, never by
       file count.
    C1  the per-query winner DIFFERS across corpora at matched size.
        Size alone does not explain the fastapi result; contamination
        remains the candidate explanation.
    C2  the per-query winner is the SAME on both corpora at matched
        size. The matched comparison does not support the
        corpus-identity explanation, and the report says so.
    C3  no pair falls inside the band on measurement, so the test does
        not run and nothing is concluded from it.
    THE C-SERIES IS NOT PAIRED AND NO INTERVAL IS COMPUTED ACROSS IT:
    the two corpora have different questions, different n (10 or 40 on
    fastapi against 37 here) and no correspondence between rows. It is
    a described comparison of point estimates, and the CSV carries
    that in a column, not only in prose.

  STATISTICAL PINS, NOT ANALYST CHOICES ON THE DAY. WS6C_BOOT_N =
  10,000, WS6C_BOOT_SEED = 42 and WS6C_ALPHA = 0.05 are
  reused FROZEN from WS6c. A cell that resolves U3 may NOT be
  re-resolved at a looser alpha, a different seed or a larger resample
  count. Seed and alpha sensitivity may be REPORTED as a sensitivity
  check, labelled as such, and it never displaces the committed
  branch.

  MULTIPLICITY, DISCLOSED RATHER THAN CORRECTED. The U-series is NINE
  UNADJUSTED CELLS at alpha = 0.05 -- 3 contrasts x 3 sizes,
  each resolved against its own 95% interval with no multiplicity
  correction. Under a global null that is ~0.45 expected U1/U2
  resolutions by chance across the nine. The correction is
  deliberately not applied, because choosing one after seeing how many
  cells resolved is a worse analyst degree of freedom than declaring
  the unadjusted rate here. A SINGLE ISOLATED U1 OR U2 AMONG NINE
  CELLS IS THEREFORE NOT EVIDENCE OF ANYTHING and the report must not
  present one as a finding; only a coherent pattern across sizes in
  one contrast may be discussed, and even then as a pattern, not as
  nine independent tests.

  UNDERPOWERED BY DESIGN, SAID IN ADVANCE (spec section 4.1). WS6c
  Phase 3 at n = 40 reached P3 on its primary comparison -- the
  interval missed excluding zero by 85 tokens. WS9's per-size cells
  are n = 37 on a smaller span, so U3 at every size is the EXPECTED
  outcome and is not a disappointment. BINDING COROLLARY: the report
  may state the curve and its point estimates; it may not say the
  index wins at a size whose U-cell resolved U3.

THE PANEL. Eligibility is decided ONCE, at the smallest scope, from
gold file paths alone: a question is eligible iff EVERY path in its
expected_files lies inside S (src/). 37 of WS6c's 40 qualify; q09
(examples/), q30 (tools/) and q31 (tests/) do not. The same 37 run at
every size, so no question enters or leaves the panel as the corpus
grows -- a curve whose points are drawn over different question
populations has a shape that is partly a composition artifact.

THE SUBSETS. Nested directory tiers, decided from the tree before any
WS9 row existed. S = src/; M = the whole repository MINUS tests/;
L = the whole repository. tests/ is 51.2% of this corpus's
tiktoken_cl100k tokens (53.2% of its count_tokens) in ONE directory,
so splitting it out is the only division of this tree that gives even
log spacing. The spec's pre-registered tiering figure is the
tiktoken_cl100k share; the count_tokens share is the one the x-axis
below is drawn on. Measured count_tokens:
  S:  1,139,248 tokens,   49 files
  M:  2,017,476 tokens,  172 files
  L:  4,307,363 tokens,  342 files

THE TREES. Each size point runs against data/ws9_subset_<scope>, cut
from the DERIVED tree data/ws6c_tree_agentic_hil and sha256-verified
against it file by file. The derived tree is the pinned checkout minus
its root-level .dockerignore, which makes the pinned package index
ZERO files while reporting success -- so each subset inherits that fix
and is provably a subset of the exact corpus WS6c measured.
claude-context indexes, and the agentic arm greps, that same absolute
path.

L IS REUSED, NOT RE-RUN. The L rows come from the frozen
results/ws6c/runs.csv filtered to the panel and to these three arms.
NOT from topk_runs.csv: that file is WS6c Phase 1, 40 indexed_topk3
rows on FASTAPI with WS6a-style qids.

INTERLEAVING. claude-context builds its tool list at session entry, so
binding search_code's limit needs a second MCP session. Each size is
therefore two sequential execute_run calls against ONE checkpoint:
('indexed', 'agentic') first, then ('indexed_topk3',). The two indexed
arms are NOT interleaved with each other. Within a session the arms
are round-robined and EACH ARM GETS ITS OWN SHUFFLE of the panel,
keyed on the seed AND the arm name -- see interleaved_order in
benchlib/code_retrieval/bench.py -- so no arm sees questions in
authoring order and time-of-day variance in API latency cannot bias
one arm. Every session runs against the same index.

NO STUFFED ARM AT ANY SIZE. This corpus measures 4,307,363
count_tokens against a 960,000-token prefix budget -- 4.49x over --
so the arm is INFEASIBLE, not omitted. prefix_text is None throughout.

THE SMOKE CHECK SHARES THIS CHECKPOINT, so its rows are re-used rather
than re-billed and are the first 12 of the 222. It is a MECHANISM
check only -- both indexed arms must show turns > 1, and EVERY
search_code frame in the bound arm must return AT MOST 3 results
(check_topk_bind asserts 'never more than K', not 'exactly K': a small
corpus can legitimately return fewer, while a frame ABOVE K means the
wrapper binding did not take) -- and its outcome may drive only stop
or continue. It may not change the panel, the arms, the subsets, the
branches or the budget.

THE GOVERNOR IS WS9-SCOPED, AND THIS IS A DELIBERATE DEVIATION (spec
section 7.1). agent_harness.total_spent_across_checkpoints deliberately
fails closed across BOTH WS6a and WS6c checkpoints; seeding WS9's
governor from it would start WS9 at ~$70 already spent against a $55
cap and trip on the first call. WS9 therefore gets its own accumulator
over data/ws9_cache/*.jsonl, CUMULATIVE ACROSS WS9'S OWN PHASES (smoke,
S, M) so that no script gets a fresh budget -- that per-script reset is
the I-2 defect the original comment describes and it is not being
reintroduced. The justification is that WS9 is a separately authorised
$55 budget, not a later phase of WS6a's $75 or WS6c's $45.
